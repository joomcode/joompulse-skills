# Import vs buying domestically

Decide whether importing a product beats sourcing it locally. Two halves: the demand side,
which JoomPulse answers well, and the cost side, which it cannot see at all.

## What to ask for

- **The product** — name, SKU or EAN.
- **Landed cost if importing** — the factory or supplier price **including duties, taxes and
  delivery into Brazil**. Not the factory price alone; the factory price is the most commonly
  quoted and least useful number in this analysis.
- **Domestic cost**, if the seller has a local supplier to compare against.

If the seller does not have a landed cost, say the economics half cannot be completed and
deliver the demand half. Do not estimate import costs — duties, freight and taxes vary too
much for a guess to be responsible.

## Method

1. **Confirm demand exists.** Via JoomPulse, retrieve for the product and its comparable
   listings: sales volume, the typical selling price, revenue per listing, and how many
   sellers compete. If demand is not there, the cost comparison is moot — stop and say so.
2. **Establish landed cost.** Use the seller's figure. If the exact product cannot be
   imported, a **closely similar product's** landed cost is a reasonable approximation —
   label it as such.
3. **Run both scenarios through the same fee model.** Apply
   [margin-and-fees.md](margin-and-fees.md) twice — once with the import cost, once with the
   domestic cost — so the comparison is like-for-like. Comparing an import cost against a
   domestic *price* is the usual mistake here.
4. **Decide** using the four checks: healthy margin, proven volume, market price comfortably
   above cost, beatable competition.

Importing typically buys margin at the cost of lead time, minimum order quantity and capital
tied up in stock. If the margin gain is small, say that the operational cost probably is not
worth it — that is a legitimate verdict.

## Output

- **Verdict first:** import, buy domestically, or neither — with the reason in one line.
- The demand evidence: volume, price band, competition.
- A side-by-side margin comparison of the two sourcing routes, each labelled with which
  figures were supplied by the seller and which were estimated.
- **What is missing.** If landed cost was absent or approximated from a similar product, say
  so explicitly — the verdict is only as good as that number.
- The risks that do not appear in the margin: lead time, minimum order, currency exposure,
  and stock risk if demand is seasonal.

State the estimate disclaimer once. Show `—` for unavailable values.

## Boundaries

- **Import prices and landed-cost estimation are not available here.** No sourcing platform
  pricing, no duty calculator. The seller supplies the cost or the analysis stays on the
  demand side.
- **Never invent an import cost, duty rate or freight figure.**
- Currency movement is not modelled. If the margin depends on a thin spread, note that
  exchange-rate movement could erase it.
