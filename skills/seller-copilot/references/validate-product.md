# Validate a product before investing

A fast go / caution / no-go on one candidate product, before the seller commits capital.
This is a **gate**, not a deep study — the point is to fail fast and cheaply.

## What to ask for

- **The candidate** — a product title, keyword, listing ID, SKU or EAN. Anything that
  identifies it well enough to find comparable listings.
- **Target price**, and **unit cost** if known, for the economics half.

If the seller identifies the product only by photo, say that image identification is not
available here and ask for a name, SKU or EAN. Once identified by name, everything below
works normally.

## Method — two axes

Judge demand against saturation. Neither alone decides it.

| Demand (money in the niche) | Saturation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| High | Low–Medium | **Strong opportunity** — real demand with room to enter |
| High | High | **Possible, but you need a clear edge** — price, content, differentiation or ads |
| Low | Low | **Small niche** — only worth it if margins are strong |
| Low | High | **Avoid** — little to win, many competitors |

High demand plus high saturation is the case sellers most often misread as good. It is
viable *only* with a stated edge — so name the edge, or return caution.

## Procedure

1. **Demand and competition.** Via JoomPulse, retrieve for the candidate and its
   comparable listings: estimated revenue and sales volume, the **full price distribution**
   and where sales concentrate, how many sellers compete, and how concentrated the market is
   (whether a few sellers hold most of it).
2. **Economics.** Apply [margin-and-fees.md](margin-and-fees.md) at the target price to get
   net margin after marketplace fees. If the seller has no unit cost to give, say the
   economics half is incomplete and give the demand verdict only — do not guess the cost.
3. **Competitive read**, where the first pass is borderline: seller reputation and medals in
   the set, whether catalogue listings or independent sellers dominate, fulfilment adoption,
   and whether official stores hold the top positions. Any of these can turn a "possible"
   into an "avoid".
4. **Verdict.** Combine demand, saturation and economics into a clear **go / caution /
   no-go**, with the reason stated in one line.

## Output

- **The verdict first**, with a confidence level — before any table.
- The two axes and where the candidate falls.
- The supporting figures: revenue, volume, competitor count, concentration, price band.
- The margin at the target price, marked as an estimate unless the seller supplied real
  costs.
- **What is missing**, explicitly. A verdict reached without a unit cost is a
  demand verdict, not a full one; say so.
- If the verdict is *go*, name the single most useful next step — usually a closer look at
  the competitors ([discover-competitors.md](discover-competitors.md),
  [competitor-profile.md](competitor-profile.md)) or a pricing plan
  ([pricing.md](pricing.md)).

State the estimate disclaimer once. Show `—` for unavailable values.

## Boundaries

- **Image-based identification is unavailable.** Ask for a name, SKU or EAN.
- **Margin here is an estimate** reconstructed from published fee schedules plus
  seller-supplied cost, not a figure from Mercado Livre's own calculator.
- **Return and refund rates are not available** in JoomPulse. If the seller's decision
  hinges on return risk, say the data does not exist here rather than substituting a proxy.
- Do not turn a caution into a go because the seller wants one. The value of a gate is that
  it sometimes says no.
