# Find new products to sell

Filter the market down to a **validated shortlist** of candidate products — not a list of
bestsellers. Bestsellers are usually the most contested shelves; the goal here is demand the
seller can actually win.

## What to ask for

- **A seed** — a category, niche or keyword. If the seller is open to anything, start from
  high-opportunity categories ([category-evaluation.md](category-evaluation.md)).
- **New niche or adjacent** to what they already sell.
- Optional: price range they are comfortable with, minimum rating, national vs imported,
  target margin.

## Method

**1. Scope the category.** Start from the seller's main category, or a high-opportunity one.

**2. Filter to promising listings.** Via JoomPulse, narrow to listings that are:
- **Not official-store or pure catalogue listings** — those are hard to displace.
- **Rated 4–5.** Below 4 usually signals a product-quality problem the seller would inherit.
- **Recently listed** — roughly the last 30 to 60 days. New enough to still be accelerating,
  rather than locked up by long-established sellers. This filter matters more than it looks.
- **Inside the seller's price comfort zone.**
- **Above a minimum monthly sales floor**, to drop zero-traction items.

**3. Investigate each candidate.**
- **Price direction:** stable or rising is fine; falling suggests a race to the bottom —
  avoid.
- **Who sells it:** an entrenched leader, or a weak and beatable field?

**4. Apply the four checks.** A candidate qualifies only if it passes **all four**:

1. **Healthy positive margin** after every fee — see
   [margin-and-fees.md](margin-and-fees.md)
2. **Proven sales volume** across comparable listings, not one or two isolated sales
3. **Market price comfortably above the seller's cost**
4. **Beatable competition** — on price, content, listing quality or reputation

Discard anything that fails even one. A shortlist that passes three of four checks is not a
shortlist; it is a list of things that will disappoint.

## Seeding from outside the marketplace

If the seller is open to anything, or chasing an event or trend, candidate niches can be
seeded before touching marketplace data — from annual seasonality, scheduled events
(holidays, Carnaval, sporting events, releases), sudden viral trends, or, where the Semrush
integration is connected, rising search niches for Brazil.

External signals see demand **before** the marketplace does. Always cross-check each one
against real marketplace opportunity before recommending it, and **label which signal came
from where**. A high external signal combined with low marketplace saturation is the real
find; a high external signal with a crowded shelf is just news.

## Procedure

1. Read category-level opportunity, concentration and saturation for the seed.
2. Retrieve candidate listings ranked by recent sales volume, applying the step-2 filters.
3. Apply **opportunity logic**, not raw volume: high demand *with* low-to-medium
   concentration and low saturation.
4. Check each candidate's price trajectory, then enrich with seller reputation, review
   counts, subcategory and the longer-term trend.
5. Run the four checks and discard the failures.
6. If the seller wants a firm go/no-go on a survivor, hand it to
   [validate-product.md](validate-product.md).

## Output

The shortlist, with the four checks shown per candidate so the seller can see *why* each one
survived. Lead with how many candidates were screened and how many passed. Colour or flag
the opportunity level. Label any externally-seeded candidate with its source. Offer the full
list or a spreadsheet export when more rows exist than shown.

State the estimate disclaimer once. Show `—` for unavailable values.

## Boundaries

- **Landed import cost is not available here.** For imported candidates, treat cost as
  seller-supplied — see [import-vs-domestic.md](import-vs-domestic.md).
- **A stock budget does not translate into units.** A budget constrains the seller's **unit
  cost**, which the marketplace does not show — only selling prices. Use selling price as an
  upper-bound proxy, say that is what you did, and ask for unit cost before implying how many
  units the budget buys or what margin it leaves.
- **Daily product tracking is not available.** This is a point-in-time screen; ongoing
  monitoring needs a fresh run and a previous snapshot to compare against.
- Kits and bundles are harder to read than single items — say so rather than treating a kit
  like a simple SKU.
- Never present a bestseller list as a shortlist. If every candidate failed the checks, say
  that plainly; an empty shortlist is a real answer.
