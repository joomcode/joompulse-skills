# Discover competitors

Find who actually competes with the seller, and in what way. A single search route always
misses rivals — each of the routes below surfaces competitors the others do not.

## What to ask for

- **The seller's category**, a seed product, or their own store.
- Whether they want a **competitor set tailored to their store**, or simply a **ranked list of
  the biggest sellers in a category**. For the latter, the **top-sellers-in-category** skill
  in this repo does it directly and more cheaply — use that instead.

## The four routes

1. **By category.** Go to the **deepest, most specific** category available — specificity is
   what makes a competitor direct rather than incidental. Open its sellers, sort their products
   by revenue, and confirm they sell something genuinely similar.
2. **By product.** Search products within the category, **exclude official stores** (unless
   the seller is one), sort by revenue, and add the high-revenue sellers behind them.
3. **By seller attribute.** Filter sellers by medal or reputation tier, location, sales,
   revenue, trend, or brand — this catches rivals whose listings did not surface above.
4. **By image** — surfaces *hidden* competitors: high revenue but not ranking top. **Image
   search is not available here**; say so rather than skipping it silently, because this route
   is where hidden competitors come from.

## Segment the result

Do not hand back an undifferentiated list. Classify each competitor:

- **Direct** — sells substantially the same products
- **Potential** — adjacent assortment, could move in
- **Hidden** — earns well without ranking prominently
- **Growth leader** — **rising fast from a small base.** These matter most: they catch demand
  shifts before the established leaders do, and they are the early warning.

Note which route found each competitor — it tells the seller something about how they compete.

## Procedure

1. **Public route (no store connection needed):** routes 1, 2 and 4 — category and product
   search with official stores excluded, plus seller-attribute filtering on medal, location,
   revenue and trend.
2. **Connected-store route:** the richer competitor matrix needs the seller's shop or listing
   identifier. If it is unavailable, stay on the public route and say which was used.
3. Rank by revenue, confirm product similarity, then tag segment and trajectory.

## Output

The competitor set, grouped by segment, with the discovery route noted per competitor. Lead
with how many were found and which one matters most, and why.

Flag the fast-growth sellers explicitly — they are the finding a seller is least likely to
already know.

State the estimate disclaimer once. Show `—` for unavailable values.

Hand off for depth to [competitor-profile.md](competitor-profile.md) (one seller in detail) or
[assortment-and-price-gaps.md](assortment-and-price-gaps.md) (what they sell and at what
price).

## Boundaries

- **Image search is not available.** The hidden-competitor route cannot be run here — name the
  gap so the seller knows the set may be incomplete.
- The connected-store competitor matrix requires an identifier; a public comparison is not the
  same thing and should not be presented as one.
- Revenue and sales figures are estimates. A competitor's ranking is an estimate of an
  estimate — do not overstate small differences between adjacent sellers.
